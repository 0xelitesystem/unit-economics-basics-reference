# Unit Economics Basics

A plain-language reference on unit economics: the profit and cost of a single unit of a business, whether that unit is one customer, one order, or one subscription. It covers contribution margin, the cost to acquire a customer, the value a customer brings over time, the ratio between them, payback period, and why these numbers decide whether a business can scale.

## The core idea

Unit economics is the revenue and cost associated with one unit of a business, used to judge whether each sale or customer actually makes money. The building blocks are contribution margin (revenue per unit minus its variable costs), customer acquisition cost or CAC (what it costs to win a customer), and lifetime value or LTV (the total profit a customer brings before leaving). A business with healthy unit economics earns more from each customer than it spends to acquire and serve them, which is what lets growth add profit rather than multiply losses. When the unit economics are broken, scaling only makes the hole deeper.
