# What unit economics is

Unit economics is the revenue and costs tied to a single unit of a business, used to see whether each unit makes or loses money. A unit is the basic repeatable thing the business sells or serves, often one customer, one order, or one subscription. By zooming in to that single unit, unit economics reveals whether the core engine of the business is profitable before scale and overhead blur the picture.

## Defining the unit

The first step is choosing what one unit means for a given business. For a subscription service it is usually one customer or one account; for a marketplace it might be one transaction; for a product company it might be one order or one item sold. The right unit is the one that repeats and drives the business. Getting this definition right matters because every later calculation, cost, margin, value, is measured per that unit, and a fuzzy unit produces fuzzy conclusions.

## Why zoom in on one unit

A company's overall financials mix together fixed costs, overhead, marketing, and the underlying economics of its product, which can hide whether the core sale is profitable. By isolating a single unit and asking whether that one unit earns more than it costs, you strip away the noise. If one customer is unprofitable, a million of them will not fix it; the loss simply multiplies. Unit economics exposes that truth early, before growth disguises it as a temporary problem.

## What it tells you

Healthy unit economics mean the business earns more from each unit than it spends to create, acquire, and serve it, so growth compounds profit. Broken unit economics mean each unit loses money, so growth compounds losses. This is the central diagnostic: it tells you whether scaling is a path to profit or a faster route to running out of money. Before judging a business by its growth rate, the sound question is whether each unit, on its own, actually pays.
