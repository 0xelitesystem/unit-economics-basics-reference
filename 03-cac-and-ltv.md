# CAC and LTV

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is what a business spends to win one new customer, and lifetime value (LTV) is the total profit that customer generates before they leave. Together they answer a basic question: does a customer bring in more than they cost to acquire. These two figures are the core inputs that turn contribution margin into a verdict on the whole customer relationship.

## Customer acquisition cost

CAC is the total cost of acquiring a customer, divided across all the customers won. It includes marketing spend, advertising, sales salaries and commissions, and the other costs of attracting and converting buyers. If a company spends a sum on marketing and sales over a period and gains a number of customers, CAC is that spend divided by that number. CAC matters because customers are rarely free to acquire, and a business that overpays to win each one can grow quickly while quietly bleeding money.

## Lifetime value

LTV is the total profit a customer produces over the entire time they stay, not just from their first purchase. For a subscription, it is the contribution margin per period multiplied by how many periods the customer stays before churning. For a repeat-purchase business, it is the margin per order times the number of orders over the relationship. LTV captures that a customer is worth more than a single transaction, because retained customers keep paying. The longer customers stay and the more they spend, the higher the LTV.

## Why both are needed

Neither number means much alone. A low CAC looks great until you learn customers leave immediately, giving a tiny LTV. A high LTV looks great until you learn each customer cost a fortune to acquire. The insight comes from comparing them: what a customer is worth over their life against what they cost to obtain. CAC and LTV are deliberately paired because the relationship between them, not either figure by itself, is what reveals whether the customer engine is sound.
